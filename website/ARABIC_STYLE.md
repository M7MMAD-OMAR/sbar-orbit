# Arabic editorial conventions

Use clear Modern Standard Arabic for readers across Arabic-speaking countries. Do not use regional dialects. Rewrite the intended meaning rather than translating English word order. Prefer short, direct sentences and consistent technical terms.

Do not use vowel marks, tanween, shadda, superscript alef, or long dashes. Preserve letters such as hamza and alef with hamza. These requirements apply to visible copy, accessible labels, metadata, and error pages.

Use "وكيل" for agent, "مدير الجلسات" for broker, and "نافذة المتابعة" for viewer. Keep commands and product identifiers unchanged. Explain prerequisites and actual command results without implying automatic actions.

References reviewed:

- W3C, Arabic and Persian Layout Requirements: https://www.w3.org/TR/alreq/
- Microsoft, Arabic Localization Style Guide: https://download.microsoft.com/download/5/6/d/56d1774f-ac3f-4ad9-80af-a6dfd5df419d/ara-sau-StyleGuide.pdf
- Khallaf and Sharoff, Towards Arabic Sentence Simplification via Classification and Generative Approaches (2022): https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.09292

The research studies automatic sentence simplification, not the effectiveness of this website's wording. Microsoft's regional and vowel-mark conventions are not adopted; the user's neutral-language and no-diacritics requirements govern this site.
